# Anonymous logo critiques, part 2

Each case is one logo submitted for review: the submitter's brief (if any), then numbered critique notes, each pinned to a region of the image (coarse zone plus approximate position from the top-left). Names are replaced with [name]. Images are not included. The critiques are often blunt and originally profane: this version keeps the judgments, drops the profanity.

## Case 0108

- Submitter's note: Hello [name]. Please tell me which option to pick and what could be improved?

Critique notes (4):

1. **middle-left** [middle-left, ~3%,60% from top-left]: That this is a diagram of a car that's temporarily run off the road is only obvious to people already in on it. On top of that, the image itself just isn't appealing.
2. **center** [center, ~39%,60% from top-left]: One of the main principles of good composition is broken here — "there should be a single focal point." And even I can barely place the pixel-game graphics reference.

The rhythm of the two T's and the lane-marking stripes is enough for the composition; no need to overcomplicate it. This mark is for a café, not a pit stop. Let it be simple and expressive.

## Case 0135

- Submitter's note: I once made a logo for a jazz festival. Tell me what's good, what's bad, and what to pay attention to. Thanks.

Critique notes (7):

1. **top-left** [top-left, ~25%,5% from top-left]: At first glance the lettering is likable — it's dynamic, with cheerful flourishes. The flourishes tie all the letters into a single word and carry the reader's eye through to the end. But a moment later, various flaws and imperfections in the lettering start to show. For instance, the word is uneven in tone. You can check this by squinting: the start of the word looks darker, the middle stretch lighter, and the "р" darker again.
2. **top-left** [top-left, ~12%,26% from top-left]: The next thing I don't like is that the flourish on the "Д" ends in a sharp point.
3. **top-right** [top-right, ~72%,21% from top-left]: You can see that the connecting strokes vary too much in thickness.
4. **top-right** [top-right, ~78%,31% from top-left]: It's noticeable that letterforms that should look similar are drawn in fundamentally different ways — for example, the tops of the "р" and the "т."
5. **middle-right** [middle-right, ~80%,65% from top-left]: The flourish ends unconvincingly.
6. **bottom-left** [bottom-left, ~28%,72% from top-left]: The letter "а" fits into the space left for it, but feels out of place there.
7. **bottom-center** [bottom-center, ~36%,90% from top-left]: A pile of small, unfinished details — which is ultimately what typography is made of — are what sink the lettering.
