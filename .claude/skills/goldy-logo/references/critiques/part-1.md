# Anonymous logo critiques, part 1

Each case is one logo submitted for review: the submitter's note (if any), then numbered critique notes, each pinned to a region of the image (a coarse zone plus an approximate position measured from the top-left corner). Names have been replaced with [name]. Images are not included. The original notes were written in Russian and were often blunt or profane; here they keep their bluntness and every concrete point, with the vulgarity replaced by plain statements of judgment.

---

## Case 0001

Submitter's note: [name], good afternoon. On November 5 you reviewed a piece of work that is essentially copied from the Budweiser logo. In that case, please give your comments on the original logo. Thank you.

Critique notes (2):

top-left [top-left, ~22%,2% from top-left]: I don't see much similarity between the Budweiser logo and the one you say was copied from it. An underline beneath the word and a shared first letter aren't enough to call one a copy of the other. The Budweiser logo follows the tradition of beer logos: bold italic lettering. Its color is even, with no clogged joints or light patches. It has an expressive letter "B". The whole word leans cheerfully toward the upper right corner.
top-left [top-left, ~24%,19% from top-left]: That said, some questions do come up: why does the first "e" have such a heavy join to the "i", and why have the "s" and "e" merged the way they have. But overall it's a charming beer logo.

## Case 0030

Submitter's note: This is a logo for a project bringing together various educational and community events — lectures, workshops on different topics (psychology, photography, music, and so on). A question came up about how the mask and the logotype itself go together — the mask sits on stakes. How can this be improved without killing the letters?

Critique notes (1):

bottom-left [bottom-left, ~29%,76% from top-left]: Graphically the mask is good (I don't know how appropriate it is as an identifier for your project). But an equally elaborate logotype added on top of it is one thing too many. If you design the logotype as decoratively as the symbol, you have to apply the same principles to both — you can't mix approaches, since using two different treatments for one mark is simply too much. That means the logotype would need not just to be made of dark and light shapes (which it already is) but also symmetrical and geometrically precise, exactly like the mask. That's hard to pull off. The simplest solution is to make the logotype plain, in a clear, clean typeface, with no extra flourishes.
