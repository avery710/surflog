# Anonymous logo critiques, part 3

Each case is one logo submitted for review: the submitter's brief (if any), then numbered critique notes, each pinned to a region of the image (coarse zone plus approximate position from the top-left). Names are replaced with [name]. Images are not included. The original text is in Russian and often profane; here it is rendered into plain English with the profanity removed but the judgments kept as blunt as they were.

## Case 0238

- Submitter's note: In December 2015 a competition was held to develop symbols for the upcoming celebration of Irkutsk's 355th anniversary. In total, 151 entries were accepted across four categories:
— "Architecture of Irkutsk,"
— "Irkutsk and Baikal,"
— "Irkutsk and its Residents,"
— "Open Theme." This is the work by [name] Petrov, the winner of the public vote. What do you think? I think our residents have a taste problem.

Critique notes (5):

1. **top-left** [top-left, ~23%,1% from top-left]: Looking at this logo, you want to believe this wasn't the best entry submitted to the competition, because this entry is bad. If the residents of Irkutsk voted for it, that's a shame too.
2. **top-left** [top-left, ~28%,8% from top-left]: It's weak both in terms of meaning and graphically. The attempt to cram as many local landmarks into the logo as possible is pointless. First, Baikal is already shown as the lake's silhouette, and there's a hint of water in the wave cutting through the lettering, plus the local endemic species — the Baikal seal — is depicted, plus the word "Baikal" appears above the word "Irkutsk." And there's a blue stripe behind the seal too. There's no need to say the same thing five times: an image of Baikal or the seal alone would be enough.
3. **top-left** [top-left, ~33%,22% from top-left]: What does the phrase "the only one on the planet" refer to — Irkutsk, Baikal, or the seal?
4. **top-center** [top-center, ~35%,28% from top-left]: A cheerful seal is celebrating 355 years?
5. **top-left** [top-left, ~29%,32% from top-left]: Graphically, all of this looks like a grab-bag of tricks from someone who's learned to use illustration software: an outline tracing the whole logo, a little shadow under the seal's tail, meaningless overlapping of text and images on top of each other, a mix of typefaces.
