## Case 0673

- Submitter's note: I'll post this here instead of cluttering up the mailbox with a pointless reply to a job posting.
What do you think of Gazprom's new logo?

Critique notes (2):

1. **top-left** [top-left, ~13%,0% from top-left]: The "small made-up constraints" in the job posting ([—]) weren't described just for fun — they're there specifically to weed out people like the person asking this question. The thing is, in the real world there are always constraints, just not made-up ones: "yes, it's 2019, but the screen can only display an 800×600 image at 3 fps, and all the beauty has to be adapted to exactly those parameters," for instance. Or "the type size of the MCC station names can't be smaller than the type size of the metro station names, and all the stations under construction through 2020 also need to be marked." Or "the plastic we need is expensive, so the design file has to be redone for production in a cheaper plastic" — every day, on every project, these kinds of temporary difficulties come up, and the designer has to overcome them and still make something good, beautiful, and satisfying under real working conditions, not on Behance. Constraints are often a client's request or requirement. You can and should push back on a client — more on that another time — but if a client asks for a blue color and the letter G, there's absolutely no reason not to try making a logo in blue with the letter G. And then make another version too, if you feel like it, and explain why it's better. The job posting only constrained the image's width — you could have written a whole long-read and illustrated every train of thought.
2. **bottom-left** [bottom-left, ~14%,88% from top-left]: The mark itself is bad (the Bézier curves in particular aren't nice).
