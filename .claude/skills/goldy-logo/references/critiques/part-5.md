# Anonymous logo critiques, part 5

Each case is one logo submitted for review: the submitter's brief (if any), then numbered critique notes, each pinned to a region of the image (coarse zone plus approximate position from the top-left). Names are replaced with [name]. Images are not included. The critiques are candid and often blunt — this file keeps that directness while removing profanity.

## Case 0500

- Submitter's note: In early September 2016 the Financial University officially got a new logo: the order was signed by Rector M.A. Eskindarov on September 8, 2016. The new mark is a stylized image carrying two meanings at once — the "Conquerors of Space" monument at VDNKh, which has long served as the university's unofficial symbol, and a chart showing positive growth. The logo's colors are, of course, the colors of the Russian flag, since the university operates under the Government of the Russian Federation. At the base of the mark is the number 1919, the university's founding year.
What do you think of the new logo?

Critique notes (3):

1. **bottom-left** [bottom-left, ~28%,76% from top-left]: Here's an example of a video with a proper roasting: https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC7FkqjV8SU5I8FCHXQSQe9Q Some people put more care into designing a chair than others put into a university logo commissioned by the government.
2. **top-center** [top-center, ~56%,5% from top-left]: The new version: the idea is passable as a starting point and could be developed, but the level of craft in the details has dropped through the floor. The white segment disappears when viewed from a distance. The letter spacing in "Under the Government of the Russian Federation" breaks up the line. The type as a whole has clumped into an unreadable mess. The "1919" doesn't stand proud — it looks absurd.
3. **top-left** [top-left, ~8%,12% from top-left]: The old version: the idea was nothing special, but at least the execution of the details was passable by rural standards — rough, a solid C-minus, but at least it could pass for a Russian university logo of its era. ## Case 0535

- Submitter's note: The official symbol for Khabarovsk's 160th anniversary. The author's own comments:
Everything is brilliant simply because it's simple — that's the approach the designers of the anniversary symbol's concept took, combining simple, striking images and symbols. The festive logo consists of a circle, the number "160," and the word "Khabarovsk." At the heart of the celebratory symbol is a circular composition with intersecting red lines carrying graphic elements (a star, a circle, a square), filled in with shades of blue, green, yellow, red, and orange. The last of these is, arguably, the base color, as the most "summery" and "cheerful" one. The interweaving lines on the circle map out the historic center of Khabarovsk within the boundaries of "the Amur River – Leningradskaya Street" and "Cossack Hill – Lenin Street," highlighting the Amur and Ussuri boulevards as well as Grodekovsky Park. The graphic elements aren't just drawings — they're symbolic markers: the stars represent the Cliff, Cossack Hill, "City of Military Glory" Square, the railway station, the Amur Bridge, and the City Palace of Culture; the squares represent the Khabarovsk Krai government building and the mayor's office; the circles represent the monuments to Muravyov-Amursky, Dyachenko, and Khabarov, and the "Stele of Heroes" memorial. The Lenin-named stadium gets its own separate symbol (an "arena"). The designers focused on the circle shape for a reason: the circle is the primary image of unity and infinity, a sign of the absolute and of perfection (exactly the ideal our city strives toward in its development). The circle is also a widely recognized symbol of the sun, and therefore of life, joy, and celebration. The symbol's placement in the emblem matches the sun rising in the east. There are also associations built in with other festive images — a hot-air balloon, a cake, fruit. The lines and stars are meant to evoke fireworks and festive garlands. It's entirely possible that residents of this Far Eastern capital, known for their good sense of humor, will see "Planet Khabarovsk" in the anniversary symbol, or its own little globe. More on how this logo is applied: https://khabarovskadm.livejournal.com/44850.html

Critique notes (1):

1. **middle-left** [middle-left, ~7%,56% from top-left]: This is, honestly, a nightmare. Everything here is about as bad as it could possibly be: from the archaic typography, to the helpless layout, to the pitiful design of the applications, right down to the laughable backstory. But the symbolism itself wouldn't be so bad if it had simply stayed inside the CorelDRAW file of some "outdoor advertising and signage registration department." The print run is where the real tragedy lies. That's what's going to poison the taste of young Khabarovsk residents. That's exactly what makes it possible for a similarly bad style to show up again tomorrow, and again in ten years.
